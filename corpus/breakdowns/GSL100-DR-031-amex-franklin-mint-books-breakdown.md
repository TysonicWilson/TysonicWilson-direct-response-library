## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter is written and signed entirely in the voice of a partner company (American Express) rather than the actual product company (The Franklin Mint), explicitly stating that "arrangements have been concluded" between the two organizations.
**Likely Reason:** Having a trusted partner institution personally vouch for and distribute an offer, in its own voice and to its own list, transfers that partner's specific brand trust onto the offer in a way a product company writing to a rented or unfamiliar list cannot replicate.
**General Rule:** When a genuine co-branded partnership exists, consider having the partner institution's own executive write and sign the referral letter in their own institutional voice, explicitly naming the partnership arrangement, rather than having the product company write directly to the partner's customer list.
**Use When:** A genuine, formal partnership exists and the partner institution is willing to have a named executive personally endorse the offer.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** No genuine partnership exists, or the partner is unwilling to have a specific executive personally attach their name and credibility to the endorsement, in which case a borrowed-voice approach could misrepresent the actual relationship.
**Example From This Promotion:** "I am extremely pleased to tell you that arrangements have been concluded to make this unique collection available exclusively to American Express Cardmembers and established Franklin Mint collectors."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter restates its central superlative claim twice within a single short page, in different phrasing each time ("the finest collection of books published in a lifetime" and later "the finest private library one could own").
**Likely Reason:** In a very brief, single-page letter, restating the core claim in varied phrasing reinforces the message for a reader who may only skim, without requiring the letter to expand into extensive supporting detail.
**General Rule:** In short-format persuasive copy where extended argument-building is not practical, consider restating the single central claim in varied phrasing at two distinct points (e.g., opening and closing) rather than stating it only once.
**Use When:** The format is genuinely brief (a single page or less) and repetition can be achieved without feeling redundant due to varied phrasing and placement.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The format allows for fuller argument development, where repetition without new supporting evidence would read as padding rather than reinforcement.
**Example From This Promotion:** "What I believe will be the finest collection of books published in a lifetime..." followed later by "...in my opinion, the finest private library one could own."
