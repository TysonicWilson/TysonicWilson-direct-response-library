## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter introduces a single, sharp definitional distinction ("marketing is not the same as sales") as the centerpiece concept the reader will learn, before listing the full tactical curriculum.
**Likely Reason:** A memorable conceptual reframe gives the reader a mental hook that organizes and justifies the value of every subsequent tactical item in the curriculum list, making a long list of bullet points feel like parts of a coherent system rather than a disconnected grab-bag of tips.
**General Rule:** When selling education or a multi-part program, consider leading with a single, sharply stated reframe or distinction that reorganizes how the reader understands the category, then use the itemized curriculum to demonstrate that reframe in action.
**Use When:** The program genuinely does teach an integrated system or methodology, and the reframe is simple enough to state in one or two sentences.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The curriculum is genuinely a loose collection of unrelated tips, in which case forcing an artificial unifying reframe could feel unearned or gimmicky once the reader is inside the actual content.
**Example From This Promotion:** "I think the most important concept you'll learn at this Boot Camp is that marketing is not the same as sales. Sales are the end result of your marketing. Marketing is the system you set up that creates sales."
