## 8. Mechanism Analysis

The letter's stated "how it works" is a permanently single, subscriber-count-matched print run: rather than a standard limited-edition numbering scheme, the exact size of the entire 100-book edition is determined by however many people subscribe during the invitation window, after which no further copies will ever be produced, sold through dealers, or sold individually. Distribution eligibility itself is restricted to two named existing customer populations (Franklin Mint collectors, American Express Cardmembers), with no future invitations planned for anyone else, converting the mechanism from a typical "limited edition" marketing claim into an explicitly, mechanically enforced one-time-only production and distribution structure.

## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens with a proclamation about the scarcity of the opportunity itself ("Soon a very limited number of people will begin to acquire...") rather than opening with a description of the product's content or features.
**Likely Reason:** Leading with opportunity-scarcity rather than product-content immediately reframes the reader's decision from "do I want this product" to "will I be one of the few people who ever get the chance to have this," which can be a stronger motivator for an audience already primed to value exclusivity.
**General Rule:** When an offer's core appeal rests on genuine, permanent exclusivity, consider opening with a proclamation about the scarcity/rarity of the opportunity itself before describing the product's specific features or content.
**Use When:** The exclusivity is genuine and mechanically enforced, and the target audience has already demonstrated receptivity to exclusive/invitation-only offers.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The audience is cold or has no established relationship with exclusive-offer framing, in which case an opportunity-scarcity-first lead may read as confusing or unearned before any product context is established.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Soon a very limited number of people will begin to acquire a private library of the one hundred greatest books of all time -- in the most beautiful edition ever published."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter offers a genuinely flexible discontinuation policy (cancel anytime with thirty days' notice) while simultaneously and honestly reframing the consequence of using that flexibility (permanent inability to complete the collection).
**Likely Reason:** Being honest and specific about the real consequence of cancellation, rather than omitting it or softening it, builds trust in the overall offer's terms while still functioning as a natural retention device without resorting to punitive or hidden cancellation terms.
**General Rule:** When a continuity/subscription offer allows cancellation, consider stating the cancellation policy plainly and honestly, including any genuine, non-punitive consequence (such as loss of the ability to complete a themed collection), rather than either hiding the consequence or omitting the cancellation option's flexibility.
**Use When:** The stated consequence is true and non-punitive (a natural result of the collection's structure, not an artificially imposed penalty).
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The actual consequence of cancellation is punitive, hidden, or disproportionate, in which case transparent disclosure could itself become a liability rather than a trust-builder.
**Example From This Promotion:** "A subscriber may discontinue his subscription at any time upon thirty days' notice. May I point out, however, that once your subscription is discontinued it can never be reinstated -- and your private library can therefore never be complete."
