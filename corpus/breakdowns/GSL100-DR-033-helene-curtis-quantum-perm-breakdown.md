## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter reframes a single technical product feature ("works on three hair types with one short processing time") into a specific business-cost benefit ("saves stocking dozens of specialty perms... money and shelf space") aimed at the reader's role as a business owner rather than only as a technician.
**Likely Reason:** A B2B buyer (a salon owner) evaluates a product partly on technical merit but ultimately on business impact; translating the technical feature into a cost/space benefit speaks directly to the buyer's purchasing motivation.
**General Rule:** When selling a B2B/trade audience a product with a genuine consolidation or simplification feature, explicitly translate that feature into the specific operational costs (inventory, space, time, labor) it reduces, rather than stopping at the technical description.
**Use When:** The product genuinely replaces or consolidates multiple existing costs the buyer already tracks.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The audience is an end consumer rather than a business buyer, in which case operational-cost framing (shelf space, inventory) would be irrelevant and a different benefit framing is needed.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Most importantly, Quantum can actually save Mary's Beauty Salon from stocking dozens of specialty perms for different hair types. And that can save money and shelf space."

### Principle

**Observation:** The free-sample offer is explicitly personalized and scarcity-framed as "reserved for Mary's Beauty Salon," with a deadline tied to release of the reserved perms "to another salon" if not redeemed.
**Likely Reason:** Framing a mass sample-drop offer as an individually reserved allotment increases perceived personal relevance and urgency compared to a generic "free sample available" offer.
**General Rule:** When distributing a free trial broadly, consider framing each recipient's allotment as personally reserved and subject to reassignment if unclaimed by a deadline, to convert a generic giveaway into an individually urgent offer.
**Use When:** The offer can genuinely be tracked and reserved per-recipient (e.g., via a personalized Certificate or code).
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The claim of individual reservation would be misleading if the offer is not actually limited or trackable per recipient.
**Example From This Promotion:** "the 3 free Quantum waves (an $8.95 normal value!) reserved for you -- before we are forced to release your perms to another salon."
