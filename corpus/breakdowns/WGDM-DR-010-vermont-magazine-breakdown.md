**Big Idea:** Vermonters already share a private, affectionate running joke about the state's minor seasonal miseries (February cold, mud-season tire ruts, August black flies) — and that same shared identity and local knowledge is exactly what VERMONT MAGAZINE, written by Vermonters for Vermonters, exists to serve, replacing what used to require "know[ing] a lot of folks" or joining "the mailing list of every organization" just to find out what's really happening in your own state.

**Big Promise:** Handy, down-to-earth, in-depth journalism across six explicitly named identity-based interest categories — the gourmet, the involved citizen, the adventurer, the investor, the naturalist, and the artist in the reader — going "far beyond the picture-postcards of Vermont."

## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle: Segmenting a Single Benefit Claim Into Multiple Named, Identity-Specific Subsections

**Observation:** Rather than making one general claim that the magazine "covers many topics readers care about," the letter explicitly divides its benefit case into six separately labeled subsections, each addressed directly to a distinct facet of the reader's identity ("FOR THE GOURMET IN YOU," "FOR THE INVOLVED CITIZEN IN YOU," and so on), each populated with specific, concrete content examples.
**Likely Reason:** A single reader plausibly holds several of these identities simultaneously (someone can be both "the investor" and "the naturalist" in themselves), but a generic "broad appeal" claim risks feeling diffuse and unconvincing to any one of those identities individually; explicitly naming and addressing each identity in turn lets the reader personally self-select into several relevant categories and see direct, specific proof of relevance to each.
**General Rule:** When a product genuinely serves several distinct facets of a single reader's identity or interests, consider explicitly naming and separately substantiating each facet with specific proof, rather than making one undifferentiated "something for everyone" claim.
**Use When:** The product genuinely offers distinct, substantiable value across several specific reader identities or interest areas, and the writer has concrete examples ready for each named category.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The claimed identity categories are not genuinely well-supported by specific examples (risking the segmentation feeling like padding), or when the target audience is narrow enough that a single, more focused benefit claim would be more persuasive than a broad multi-identity structure.
**Example From This Promotion:** The six "FOR THE ___ IN YOU" subheaded sections, each with specific named content examples matched to that identity.
