## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens with a single, vivid, onomatopoeic word ("Wham!!") standing alone as its own paragraph, before any explanation follows.
**Likely Reason:** A sensory, non-verbal opening device creates instant emotional recognition and curiosity (the reader must read on to understand the reference) more efficiently than an explanatory sentence would, while also standing out visually against a page of conventional prose.
**General Rule:** When the underlying problem is a shared, viscerally-felt experience common to the entire target audience, consider opening with a single vivid sensory word or sound-effect device rather than an explanatory sentence, letting the explanation follow immediately after.
**Use When:** The target problem is genuinely universal and viscerally recognizable to the audience, so the ambiguous opening resolves quickly and doesn't create confusion or frustration.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The problem or context is not immediately, universally recognizable, in which case an ambiguous single-word opening risks confusing rather than intriguing the reader.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Wham!! That's the sound of a taxpayer being hit between the eyes with the annual property tax bill."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly leverages the reader's existing account relationship with the institution ("your mortgage loan is with us. No problem.") as the primary credibility device, rather than introducing external proof.
**Likely Reason:** When pitching an ancillary product to an existing customer base, explicitly referencing the existing relationship reduces the trust-building burden the letter would otherwise need to carry through external proof, testimonials, or credentials.
**General Rule:** When cross-selling an ancillary product to an existing customer base, explicitly reference the existing account relationship as the primary reason the new offer is low-friction and trustworthy, rather than building credibility from scratch as if addressing a cold prospect.
**Use When:** The audience genuinely is an existing customer base with a real, ongoing account relationship to the institution making the offer.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The audience is a cold, unrelated prospect list, in which case there is no genuine existing relationship to reference and the device would be inapplicable or misleading.
