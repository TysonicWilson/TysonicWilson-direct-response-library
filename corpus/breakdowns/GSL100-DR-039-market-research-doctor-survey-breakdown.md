## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly and directly addresses the risk that its own token incentive (a small check) could seem disrespectful of the reader's actual time value, rather than hoping the reader doesn't notice the mismatch.
**Likely Reason:** For a high-value-time professional audience, a token incentive that goes unexplained risks being perceived as either insulting or manipulative; explicitly reframing its purpose (goodwill gesture, not payment) prevents that negative interpretation from silently suppressing response.
**General Rule:** When offering a small token incentive to a professional audience whose time is clearly worth far more than the incentive, explicitly and respectfully name that mismatch and clarify what the incentive is (and isn't) meant to represent.
**Use When:** The incentive genuinely is a token gesture rather than a serious attempt at fair compensation, and the audience is sophisticated enough to notice and evaluate the mismatch.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The incentive is intended to be perceived as genuinely fair compensation, in which case drawing attention to its smallness would undermine that framing.
**Example From This Promotion:** "The dollar check is not meant as payment for your time, [Doctor] .... we know your time is more valuable than that. The real pay-back will come in professional services that this survey will enable us to offer to you."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter physically combines the response incentive (the check) and the response instrument (the survey questions) into a single object, rather than using a separate reply card or form.
**Likely Reason:** Reducing the number of distinct physical items the reader must handle to respond lowers the effort threshold for a busy audience likely to discard multi-piece mailings.
**General Rule:** When soliciting a minimal response from a time-constrained audience, consider consolidating the incentive and the response mechanism into a single physical object to reduce handling friction.
**Use When:** The response required is genuinely minimal (e.g., a couple of short questions) and can physically fit on the incentive item itself.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The response requires more space or complexity than a small object like a check can accommodate, in which case a separate, well-designed reply form may serve better.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Will you please answer the two questions on the reverse side of the enclosed check?"
