**Secondary Lead Characteristics:** The lead also functions partly as a proclamation, since it makes an implicit bold claim (this gimmick works) before explaining or proving it.

**Likely Reader State:** A busy corporate executive sorting through a large daily mail pile, primed to discard most unsolicited sales mail unopened, whose attention has just been captured by an unusual physical sensation (feeling currency through the envelope) before reading a single word.

## 5. Structural Map

1. **Device Reveal** — "I have taken the very usual step of enclosing a $1 bill in a clear plastic envelope for a reason." *Function:* names the attention device immediately, leveraging the physical sensation already felt by the reader.
2. **Purpose Justification** — "I had to think of a way to get your attention for my letter, to make sure my letter stood out in the enormous pile of mail you must receive everyday." *Function:* frames the gimmick as a practical necessity, not a cheap trick.
3. **Ethical Framing** — "I would never do something this gimmicky if I were not writing to you about an important matter to you and your company. But I am a marketing professional. That's how I make my living." *Function:* preemptively defends the device's legitimacy and identifies the writer's profession.
4. **Credibility / Track Record Claim** — "after more than 19 years of experience, that enclosing a $1 bill with my letter... almost always increases response to my sales and marketing letters by 50% or more." *Function:* uses the device itself as proof of the writer's broader expertise.
5. **Value Proposition** — "I believe I have a way to significantly improve your marketing, perhaps improve your return on investment on all your marketing efforts by 50% or more if you will give me a few minutes of your time." *Function:* generalizes the demonstrated principle into a broad, high-stakes promise for the reader's business.
6. **Meeting Request** — "I would like to come in and meet with you to talk about your marketing." *Function:* states the explicit, singular ask of the letter.
7. **Additional-Value Teaser** — "I have scores of methods and techniques that I use, besides enclosing $1 bills with my letters, that will ensure your letters and your marketing materials are opened and read by your target audience." *Function:* implies the dollar-bill trick is only one of many tools, building curiosity about the meeting's value.
8. **Proactive Follow-Up Plan** — "In the next few days, I will call your secretary, Linda Johnson, to see if I can meet with you in the next week or two." *Function:* removes the burden of response from the reader by committing to an outbound follow-up call, while showing the writer has done enough research to name the secretary.
9. **Alternative Contact Path** — "My hope is that you will let Ms. Johnson know so she will expect my call. Alternatively, you may have her call me to schedule the appointment." *Function:* offers a lower-friction path (delegate to assistant) alongside the direct one.
10. **Contact Info & Close** — direct office number and cell number provided; "I look forward to speaking with you soon." *Function:* provides multiple direct channels and closes on an assumptive, low-pressure note.

**Compressed architecture:** Device Reveal → Purpose Justification → Ethical Framing → Credibility Claim (Track Record) → Value Proposition → Meeting Request → Additional-Value Teaser → Proactive Follow-Up Plan → Contact Info → Close.

## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter uses a real, physical enclosure (currency) as both an attention-getting device and as self-demonstrating proof of its own core marketing claim.
**Likely Reason:** A claim experienced directly by the reader (they felt/saw the currency and opened the envelope because of it) is inherently more persuasive than the same claim merely asserted in prose, since the reader's own behavior has already confirmed it.
**General Rule:** When possible, design an attention-getting device that doubles as living proof of the letter's central claim, so the reader's own reaction to the device becomes evidence supporting the pitch.
**Use When:** The core claim being sold is fundamentally about attention, response, or engagement — a domain where a physical device can plausibly serve as direct proof.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The core claim is unrelated to attention/response mechanics, in which case a gimmick would capture attention without meaningfully supporting the actual argument, risking a sense of disconnect or gimmickry without substance.
**Example From This Promotion:** "I have taken the very usual step of enclosing a $1 bill in a clear plastic envelope for a reason... enclosing a $1 bill with my letter... almost always increases response to my sales and marketing letters by 50% or more."

## 17. Worth Swiping

- Using a self-demonstrating device (one that proves the pitch's core claim through the reader's own experience) rather than a purely decorative attention-getter.
- Preemptively justifying an unconventional tactic before the reader can dismiss it as a cheap trick.
- Naming a specific gatekeeper (the secretary, by name) to signal research and seriousness.
- Shifting the next-step burden to the seller's proactive follow-up call rather than requiring the reader to initiate contact.
- Minimizing the size of the initial ask ("a few minutes") relative to the size of the promised benefit.

## 18. Context-Dependent Elements

- Mailing real currency carries direct costs, potential mail-handling/security concerns, and diminishing effectiveness with overuse or imitation; it is not a costlessly repeatable tactic.
- The specific 50% response-rate figure is an unverified, self-reported statistic specific to this writer's claimed experience and should not be reused as if independently established.
- The premise assumes the recipient is a corporate executive who personally opens or is shown physical mail, a context that may not generalize to email-based or gatekeeper-filtered communication channels.
