**Big Idea:** John D. Rockefeller Sr. said he'd pay more for the ability to deal with people than for any other commodity under the sun — and this book distills the same proven Dale Carnegie course material used by thousands of business leaders (illustrated by Michael O'Neil's transformation from struggling mechanic to top salesman) into an accessible, inexpensive book.

**Big Promise:** Learn specific, actionable techniques for winning friends, influencing people, and succeeding in business and personal life — the same skills Rockefeller valued above all others.

## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle: Borrowing a Famous Authority's Specific Language to Reframe an Abstract Value

**Observation:** Rather than the copywriter asserting that "people skills are valuable," the ad quotes Rockefeller's specific, vivid framing ("as purchasable a commodity as sugar or coffee").
**Likely Reason:** A specific, quotable, and unusual comparison from a famous, credible source is more persuasive and memorable than an equivalent claim made in generic marketing language.
**General Rule:** When a product's core value proposition can be authentically tied to a specific, well-known authority figure's own words, lead with the direct quote rather than paraphrasing or asserting the claim independently.
**Use When:** A genuine, attributable, and specific quote exists that directly supports the core value claim.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** No genuine attributable quote exists, or the available quote is generic enough that paraphrasing would be equally effective without the added credibility risk of misattribution.
**Example From This Promotion:** The full Rockefeller quote opening the ad's body copy.
