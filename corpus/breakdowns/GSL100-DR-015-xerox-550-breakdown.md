## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly asks and answers the reader's most likely skeptical question ("How can Xerox bring you such a high-quality copier at such a low price?") rather than waiting for the reader to form that doubt silently.
**Likely Reason:** An unusually low price from a premium brand naturally triggers "what's the catch?" skepticism; proactively naming and transparently answering that exact question converts a potential trust-breaker into a trust-builder.
**General Rule:** When offering an unusually favorable price from an established, premium-positioned brand, explicitly surface and answer the "how is this possible?" question with a specific, causal explanation, rather than simply asserting the value and hoping skepticism doesn't surface.
**Use When:** A genuine, explainable reason for the favorable price exists (e.g., new distribution model, reconditioned inventory, bulk sourcing) that can be described honestly and specifically.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** No credible explanation exists, or the explanation itself would raise more concern than it resolves (e.g., a genuinely lower-quality substitute), in which case surfacing the question could backfire.
**Example From This Promotion:** "HOW CAN XEROX BRING YOU SUCH A HIGH-QUALITY COPIER AT SUCH A LOW PRICE? The answer is simple."

### Principle

**Observation:** The P.S. reframes the offer for a segment of readers who don't fit the letter's primary use case (needing a first copier), positioning the product instead as a secondary/backup purchase.
**Likely Reason:** Not every reader in a broad B2B mailing shares the same underlying situation; addressing a secondary audience segment in the P.S. captures additional qualified buyers without diluting the primary argument built through the rest of the letter.
**General Rule:** When a broad mailing list likely contains readers in meaningfully different situations relative to the core offer, use the P.S. to explicitly address the highest-value secondary use case, rather than assuming the primary pitch covers everyone.
**Use When:** A distinct, plausible secondary use case exists and can be addressed briefly without requiring restructuring of the main letter.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The secondary use case is a stretch or requires extensive justification, in which case it would be better addressed in the main body or a separate mailing entirely.
**Example From This Promotion:** "P.S. If you already have a copier that you're pleased with, the Xerox 550 is still a great bargain for you. It's the ideal 'second' copier on heavy volume days. And it's great as a back-up unit when your other copier is down."
