## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens by reframing an object the reader already owns (the telephone) as an incomplete version of the solution being sold, rather than presenting the product as an entirely new category of purchase.
**Likely Reason:** Starting from something the reader already owns and trusts lowers psychological resistance to the pitch, because the reader is being asked to "complete" or "upgrade" an existing asset rather than adopt an unfamiliar new category of spending.
**General Rule:** When a product genuinely extends or completes the function of something the reader already commonly owns, open by reframing that existing asset as incomplete, rather than opening with a generic need statement.
**Use When:** A genuine, credible functional connection exists between the product and something the reader already owns (as with a telephone-based alarm dialer and an existing telephone line).
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** No genuine functional connection exists, in which case the reframe would feel like a forced or misleading rhetorical trick rather than a substantiated observation.
**Example From This Promotion:** "You may not realize it, but you already have half of a security system to protect your home against such hazards as fire, smoke and burglary. It's your telephone."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly names and rejects the "standard package" framing typical of its product category, replacing it with individualized, specialist-led customization.
**Likely Reason:** In categories where competitors commonly sell standardized bundles, explicitly naming and rejecting that framing differentiates the offer by implication (competitors offer commodity packages; we offer tailored expertise) without requiring a direct competitor comparison.
**General Rule:** When most competitors in a category sell standardized packages, explicitly name and reject the "package" framing in favor of individualized, expert-led customization, even without naming specific competitors.
**Use When:** The individualized-customization claim is genuinely true and can be credibly delivered by trained personnel.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The offer is not actually meaningfully customized, in which case the claim risks appearing hollow if the reader later discovers a generic delivery process.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Because we know each home is different, we don't offer any standard security 'package.' Instead, each Phone Alarm System is individually designed to provide the exact types of protection you require."
