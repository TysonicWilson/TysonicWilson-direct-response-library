## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens with three short, parallel, relatable failure scenarios, each ending in the identical punchline ("So you find yourself saying 'Damn!'"), before naming any solution.
**Likely Reason:** Repeating the same emotional punchline across varied, quickly recognizable scenarios builds a rhythmic sense of universality and inevitability ("this happens to everyone, including you") more efficiently than a single, longer narrative would.
**General Rule:** When a product solves a recurring frustration that manifests in several distinct but related situations, consider opening with a short series of parallel scenarios sharing a repeated emotional punchline, rather than one extended story.
**Use When:** The underlying frustration genuinely recurs across multiple distinct, easily-dramatized situations relevant to the target reader.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The product addresses a single, narrow situation better served by one detailed story -- forcing artificial variety would dilute rather than strengthen the emotional hook.
**Example From This Promotion:** The three "let's say" scenarios (meeting, decision-making, investment) each closing with "So you find yourself saying 'Damn!'"

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly states the product is never discounted ("not sold at cut rates... Company policy") and uses this as a credibility/quality signal rather than treating the lack of a discount as a weakness to work around.
**Likely Reason:** Explicitly refusing to discount reframes full price as evidence of consistent quality and demand (the product doesn't need artificial inducements to sell), while also removing the reader's incentive to delay in hopes of a better future offer.
**General Rule:** When a product genuinely has no introductory-discount policy, consider stating this explicitly and framing it as a quality/integrity signal, rather than avoiding the topic or apologizing for full pricing.
**Use When:** The no-discount policy is genuinely true and consistently enforced -- readers can later verify the claim, and inconsistency would damage trust.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The brand does, in fact, run discounts elsewhere or subsequently -- discovering the claim was false would be a significant credibility breach.
**Example From This Promotion:** "As you may or may not know, subscriptions to BUSINESS WEEK are not sold at cut rates. No 'special introductory offers.' Company policy... this is it."
