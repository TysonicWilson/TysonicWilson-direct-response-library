The ad's persuasive strategy centers on positioning natural, drug-free remedies as suppressed knowledge rather than simply "alternative medicine." The cover headline literally uses the word "CENSORED" stamped over the masthead, and the sub-copy explicitly names the alleged suppressors: "greedy drug companies and FDA bureaucrats." ## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle: Framing Unattributed or Loosely-Sourced Claims as "Suppressed" Rather Than Simply Presenting Them

**Observation:** Rather than presenting natural-remedy claims as ordinary information, the piece frames the entire body of claims as actively censored by a named institutional enemy (FDA, drug companies), which is offered as the explanation for why the reader hasn't encountered this information through mainstream channels.
**Likely Reason:** A "suppression" frame preemptively answers the reader's natural skepticism question ("if this is so effective, why haven't I heard of it?") with an emotionally satisfying answer (corporate/institutional self-interest) rather than requiring the copy to independently establish scientific credibility for every individual claim.
**General Rule:** When a promotion's claims are difficult to fully substantiate with citable, mainstream sources, an "enemy is hiding this from you" frame can redirect reader skepticism away from the claims themselves and onto a distrusted institution — but this increases compliance risk in regulated categories and should be evaluated against current advertising and health-claims regulations before reuse.
**Use When:** There is a genuine, well-documented tension between a category of remedies/information and an institutional gatekeeper (patent economics, regulatory approval costs) that can be explained honestly and is not itself fabricated.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The underlying claims cannot be substantiated at all, or the category is health/medical/financial and subject to strict regulatory scrutiny — this pattern carries meaningful compliance and credibility risk if the "suppression" narrative substitutes for genuine evidence rather than supplementing it.
**Example From This Promotion:** The CoQ10 "Medical Censorship Can Cost You Your Life!" section and the repeated "the medical establishment doesn't want you to know" framing throughout.
