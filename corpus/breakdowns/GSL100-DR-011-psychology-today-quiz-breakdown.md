## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** Instead of describing the magazine's value proposition abstractly, the letter has the reader directly experience it by completing a 42-question self-assessment quiz that is then interpreted back to them.
**Likely Reason:** Experiential, self-generated proof ("I just experienced this myself") is more persuasive and memorable than being told a claim, because the reader cannot dismiss their own participation and answers as marketing spin.
**General Rule:** When a product's core value is a kind of insight, transformation, or experience, consider having the prospect experience a compressed version of that value directly within the sales piece itself, rather than only describing it.
**Use When:** The core value is genuinely demonstrable in a short, self-contained format (a quiz, a mini-assessment, a sample), and the demonstration naturally leads back to the product being sold.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The demonstration device is gimmicky, disconnected from the actual product experience, or so long/complex that it becomes a barrier rather than an engagement hook.
**Example From This Promotion:** The 42-item "Compatibility Test" quiz with a scored personality interpretation, positioned before any explicit sales pitch.

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter reframes the reader's willingness to complete the quiz as proof that they are already the exact kind of person the magazine is meant for.
**Likely Reason:** Using the prospect's own completed action (rather than an external claim) as evidence of fit for the product creates a powerful consistency effect — having just demonstrated interest in self-knowledge, declining the offer would feel psychologically inconsistent.
**General Rule:** When a prospect completes an engagement device (quiz, assessment, interactive tool), explicitly interpret their act of participation itself as evidence they belong to the target audience, before making the offer.
**Use When:** The engagement device is genuinely optional and completed voluntarily, so the consistency effect is authentic rather than coerced.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** Participation was trivial, mandatory, or low-effort enough that framing it as meaningful "proof" of fit would feel manipulative or overstated.
**Example From This Promotion:** "The fact that you allowed yourself to be tested shows that you're interested in learning more about yourself -- and that's what Psychology Today's all about."
