## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens with a single, specific, quantified, yes/no-answerable question as its entire hook, rather than building up to the offer through narrative or context.
**Likely Reason:** For a busy B2B decision-maker audience, a direct question that states the specific benefit and asks for a specific, low-commitment response respects the reader's time and clarifies the letter's purpose within the first sentence, increasing the likelihood of a quick decision to respond.
**General Rule:** When the target audience is time-constrained business decision-makers and the offer's value proposition can be reduced to a single specific, quantified claim, open with that claim as a direct question rather than building context first.
**Use When:** The letter can back the opening question with genuine, defensible substantiation immediately afterward (as this letter does with its competitive-pricing justification).
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The specific quantified claim cannot be credibly substantiated, in which case an unsupported number in the opening line risks immediate reader skepticism and disengagement.
**Example From This Promotion:** "If I could show how I can save you 20% over what you are paying now for your printing, would you be interested in meeting with me?"

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly names and directly denies the most obvious objection to its central claim (that a steep discount implies lower quality) immediately after making that claim, rather than waiting for the reader to raise it.
**Likely Reason:** Proactively naming an objection the reader is very likely to already be forming demonstrates awareness of the reader's skepticism and allows the letter to control the framing of the response, rather than leaving the reader to draw their own (potentially negative) conclusion unaddressed.
**General Rule:** When making an aggressive claim (steep discount, unusual speed, etc.) that carries an obvious, predictable objection, name that objection directly and immediately after the claim, then resolve it with a concrete justification and/or guarantee.
**Use When:** A genuine, concrete justification and/or guarantee exists to credibly resolve the named objection.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** No genuine justification or guarantee is available, in which case naming the objection without a credible resolution could actively reinforce reader doubt.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Please be assured that our lower prices in no way mean lower quality. Quite the opposite... if you ever fmd a problem with the quality of our printing, we'll print it again for you at no charge."
