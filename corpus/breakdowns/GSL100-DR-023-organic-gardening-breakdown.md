## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter externalizes the reader's likely mistakes onto a third-party photographed "friend" rather than addressing the reader's own errors directly.
**Likely Reason:** Attributing the mistakes to someone else lets the reader self-diagnose and compare their own practices without feeling personally criticized, reducing defensiveness while still delivering the same diagnostic content.
**General Rule:** When a letter needs to point out likely reader mistakes or shortcomings, consider externalizing those mistakes onto a relatable third-party example (a photograph, a composite character, a named "friend") rather than stating them as direct accusations against the reader.
**Use When:** The mistakes being described are common and non-stigmatizing enough that readers will readily recognize themselves in a third-party example without feeling singled out.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The mistake is highly personal, sensitive, or embarrassing -- an externalized device might feel evasive or insincere rather than tactful in that context.
**Example From This Promotion:** "mistakes like our friend in the color photograph on our outside envelope -- mistakes that may be causing you extra work and added frustration."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter structures its entire proof section as a numbered sequence of named mistakes, each with a specific causal explanation and corrective technique, escalating from labor-saving concerns to ecological/health concerns before being cut off mid-list ("we could go on and on").
**Likely Reason:** A numbered sequence gives the reader a trackable structure and sense of progress through the letter, while escalating from a mild concern (extra weeding labor) to a more serious one (ecological harm from poison sprays) builds momentum toward the letter's climax before deliberately leaving the list incomplete to imply greater depth available elsewhere.
**General Rule:** When a product's value rests on correcting several distinct, escalating problems, consider presenting them as a numbered sequence that builds from minor to major concerns, then explicitly signal that the list continues beyond what the letter itself covers.
**Use When:** There are genuinely multiple distinct, escalating problems the product addresses, and a deeper resource (a book, a course, a bonus) can credibly claim to cover additional items beyond the sampled ones.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The underlying problems are not genuinely escalating in seriousness, or the deeper resource does not actually contain meaningfully more content than what's already been previewed -- the "we could go on and on" claim would then be misleading.
**Example From This Promotion:** The sequence from "MISTAKE NUMBER ONE" (weeding, a labor issue) through "MISTAKE NUMBER FIVE" ("the greatest mistake of all," poison sprays, an ecological/health issue), before "MISTAKE NUMBER SIX" is deferred to the booklet.
