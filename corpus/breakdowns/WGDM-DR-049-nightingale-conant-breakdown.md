## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle 1

**Observation:** The letter pairs a historically risky, hard-to-verify legitimacy claim (6,000-year-old Ayurvedic tradition) immediately with a specific, checkable modern medical credential (a named, practicing M.D. endocrinologist) rather than relying on either alone.
**Likely Reason:** A purely historical/traditional appeal risks reading as unscientific or fringe to a modern, skeptical reader; a purely modern-authority appeal forfeits the "different from everything else you've tried" differentiation. Combining both lets each offset the other's weakness.
**General Rule:** When positioning a product as "ancient wisdom" or "suppressed knowledge," pair that historical-legitimacy claim immediately with a specific, checkable modern credential to prevent the claim from reading as unscientific to a skeptical audience.
**Use When:** The product genuinely does draw on a real historical tradition or practice, and a genuinely credentialed expert is available to front the modern-legitimacy half of the pairing.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** Either half of the pairing is unsubstantiated -- an invented historical lineage or an unverifiable/exaggerated credential undermines the entire technique and creates compliance risk, particularly in health-claim categories.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Used for more than 6,000 years, the Science of Natural Health Care, or Ayur-Veda... Dr. Deepak Chopra, M.D., a practicing endocrinologist and world leader in the field of mind/body medicine, shows you how effortless and natural permanent weight loss can be."

### Principle 2

**Observation:** The letter explicitly dismisses the three most obvious conventional authorities in the category (doctor, diet books, health spas) within the first two paragraphs, before introducing its own mechanism.
**Likely Reason:** For an audience with repeated prior category disappointment, pre-emptively naming and dismissing the exact sources of that prior disappointment signals "I know why you're skeptical" before making any new claims, which can lower resistance to the new claim that follows.
**General Rule:** When addressing an audience with known prior negative experience in a product category, name and dismiss the specific prior sources of disappointment before introducing the new solution, rather than only differentiating the new solution positively.
**Use When:** The audience genuinely has identifiable, common prior sources of disappointment (a small number of well-known conventional options) that can be named specifically and credibly dismissed.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The named "conventional authorities" (here, doctors) are ones the reader still generally trusts and relies on for other reasons -- broadly undermining trusted institutions can backfire if not carefully scoped to the specific claim at hand.
**Example From This Promotion:** "But your doctor probably won't mention it. Diet books don't discuss it. And the most exclusive health spas would quickly put themselves out of business if they revealed its amazing power."
