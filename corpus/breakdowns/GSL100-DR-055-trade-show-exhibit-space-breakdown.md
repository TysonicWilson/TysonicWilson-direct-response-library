3. **Event Basics + Early Urgency** — "The Metropolitan Cooking & Entertaining Show is being held at The Washington Convention Center on November 3-5, 2006. ## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly states it is being sent only to "a select group of companies... that are leaders in their area of specialty," despite the offer (paid exhibit space) being, in principle, available to any qualifying company that responds.
**Likely Reason:** Framing a broadly available paid opportunity as an exclusive, curated invitation increases the recipient's sense of being personally chosen rather than merely solicited, which can increase both response rate and the perceived legitimacy/prestige of participating.
**General Rule:** When soliciting participation in an event or program from a defined, identifiable target list (rather than a truly open mass audience), consider explicitly stating that the invitation is selective/curated, provided the underlying claim is honest (a genuinely defined, relevant target list, not a fabricated exclusivity claim).
**Use When:** The recipient list genuinely is a curated subset (by industry, size, geography, or other relevant criteria) rather than the general public.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The invitation is, in fact, being sent to an undifferentiated mass audience — a false exclusivity claim risks damaging trust if the recipient later discovers the "select group" was not meaningfully selective.
**Example From This Promotion:** "This letter of invitation is only being sent to owners and executives of a select group of companies in the cooking and entertaining industry that are leaders in their area of specialty."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter defers pricing until after extensive value-building content (scale, timing, programming, industry growth statistics), presenting the $12.00-per-square-foot rate only in a dedicated, late-letter "Pricing" section.
**Likely Reason:** Establishing the scope and credibility of the opportunity before revealing cost allows the reader to evaluate the price against an already-formed sense of the opportunity's scale and value, rather than anchoring first on a bare number with no context.
**General Rule:** When an offer's value case is genuinely strong and multi-faceted, build that case in full before disclosing price, so the eventual number is evaluated against an established value frame rather than in isolation.
**Use When:** The value case is substantial enough to justify the delay, and the letter is long/detailed enough that price placement can be meaningfully sequenced.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The offer is simple or the audience expects/prefers immediate price transparency — in short-format letters or highly price-sensitive contexts, delaying price disclosure can read as evasive rather than strategic.
**Example From This Promotion:** Price ("$12.00 per square foot") appears only after the event-scale, timing, programming, exhibitor-roster, and industry-growth sections, under a dedicated bolded "Pricing" subhead.

## 17. Worth Swiping

- Framing a broadly-solicited but genuinely defined-list B2B offer as a curated, selective invitation to increase perceived prestige and relevance.
- Preempting the "there's plenty of time" objection immediately after stating the event date, rather than waiting for it to surface later in the letter.
- Pairing event-specific scale claims with independently-named, broader industry-growth statistics to reassure a reader skeptical of the organizer's own attendance projections.
- Deferring price disclosure until after the full value case has been built, using a clearly labeled subhead so a skimming reader can still find it easily.
- Reframing competitor presence at the event as a reason to attend (visibility risk if absent) rather than a reason to avoid a crowded floor.
- Stacking multiple, parallel response channels (mail, phone, email, online) at the point of highest reader urgency (the P.S.) to remove any remaining friction to action.
