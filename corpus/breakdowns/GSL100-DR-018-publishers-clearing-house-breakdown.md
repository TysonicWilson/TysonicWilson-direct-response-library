## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens by crediting the reader with already understanding and accepting the core value proposition, rather than re-explaining or re-arguing it.
**Likely Reason:** For an existing, already-converted customer, re-selling the basic concept wastes attention and can feel condescending or redundant; crediting existing understanding respects the relationship and frees space for the actual current offer.
**General Rule:** In repeat-customer or house-list mailings, consider opening by affirming what the reader already knows/believes about the offer, then moving quickly into the specific, current, actionable content rather than re-arguing the underlying premise.
**Use When:** The audience is genuinely a repeat or existing customer with demonstrated prior acceptance of the core offer.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The list is actually cold or unqualified -- assuming prior understanding with a prospect who lacks it would leave the pitch unpersuasive and confusing.
**Example From This Promotion:** "It is transparently clear to you as a Clearing House customer... that you get up to twice as many copies for your magazine dollar."

**Likely Reason:** Explicitly disclaiming pressure in a repeat-mailing relationship can reduce reactance and preserve long-term list health, since customers who don't feel pressured are more likely to remain receptive to future mailings.
**General Rule:** In recurring house-list communications, consider periodically and explicitly acknowledging that not every mailing expects a purchase, to preserve trust and reduce unsubscribe/fatigue risk over a long mailing relationship.
