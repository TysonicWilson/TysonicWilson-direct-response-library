## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly disclaims a guarantee of value appreciation ("we cannot guarantee the extent to which our first plate will appreciate in value") immediately before listing multiple specific value-justification factors.
**Likely Reason:** An explicit disclaimer placed just before the strongest value claims signals restraint and honesty, which can make the surrounding value arguments feel more credible than if they were presented as unqualified promises.
**General Rule:** When making appreciation- or value-based claims about a collectible or investment-adjacent product, consider pairing the claims with an explicit, honest disclaimer about the lack of guarantee, placed close to (not far from) the claims themselves.
**Use When:** The value claims are genuinely uncertain and the disclaimer is a truthful reflection of that uncertainty.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The product's value claims are actually certain or contractually guaranteed, in which case an unnecessary disclaimer could undersell a genuinely strong claim.
**Example From This Promotion:** "While we cannot guarantee the extent to which our first plate will appreciate in value, we believe that it has a lot going for it."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter restricts eligibility for the direct-order channel explicitly to "established Franklin Mint collectors," rather than offering the same direct-order opportunity to a broader or colder list.
**Likely Reason:** Restricting an offer's access to an existing, already-invested customer base converts a standard sales pitch into a privileged, insider notification, which can increase both response rates and perceived exclusivity of the product itself.
**General Rule:** When launching a new limited-production item, consider explicitly restricting initial direct-order access to an existing, engaged customer base rather than opening the channel broadly, and state that restriction explicitly in the copy.
**Use When:** The company genuinely has an existing, engaged customer list and the restriction is genuinely enforced (not merely stated for effect).
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The company lacks a meaningfully engaged existing customer base, in which case an artificially restricted "exclusive" framing to a cold list would ring false.
**Example From This Promotion:** "we will accept no direct orders from anyone except established Franklin Mint collectors like yourself... No more than one plate can be sold to any subscriber."
