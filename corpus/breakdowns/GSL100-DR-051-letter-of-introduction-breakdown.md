## 5. Structural Map

1. **Self-Introduction** — "I'm writing to introduce myself to you. My name is [Name], and I am the new Vice President of Sales for [Company]." *Function:* establishes identity and role/authority in two short sentences before any ask.
2. **Meeting Request with Benefit Teaser** — "I would very much like to stop by next week to say hello and to show you a new [product] we've developed that I'm confident will significantly streamline your [whatsis] and cut your [whatsis] costs by at least 20% with fewer breakdowns and mechanical problems." *Function:* combines the ask (a visit) with the strongest available benefit claim in a single sentence.
3. **Persistence / Follow-Up Commitment** — "I will call next week to see if I can stop by your office, even if you're only available for ten minutes." *Function:* removes ambiguity about next steps by having the seller take ownership of follow-through, and pre-shrinks the time commitment to reduce resistance.
4. **Availability / Access Reassurance** — "In the meantime, if you need any assistance with anything, please don't hesitate to call me at [Number.] If I'm not at my desk, please feel free to call me on my cell: [Number]." *Function:* signals accessibility and service orientation ahead of any transaction, building soft goodwill.
5. **Sign-off** — "Sincerely, [Name], Vice President for Sales." *Function:* closes with title repeated for reinforced authority.

**Compressed architecture:** Self-Introduction → Meeting Request + Benefit Teaser → Persistence Commitment → Access Reassurance → Sign-off.

## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter combines the meeting request and the benefit teaser into a single sentence, then immediately shrinks the ask to "even if you're only available for ten minutes."
**Likely Reason:** Making the specific benefit and the specific (minimal) time cost visible in the same breath lets the prospect do a fast mental cost-benefit calculation without needing to read further, increasing the odds of a quick "yes" or at least non-refusal.
**General Rule:** When the goal of a letter is only to secure a first meeting (not a sale), pair the strongest available quantified benefit claim directly with an explicitly minimized time/effort ask, in the same sentence or the sentence immediately following.
**Use When:** The actual selling will happen in a later touch (a call, meeting, demo) and this letter's sole job is opening the door.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The letter itself is meant to close a sale or drive an immediate transaction — minimizing the ask this aggressively would undersell the product's actual value in that context.
**Example From This Promotion:** "I would very much like to stop by next week to say hello and to show you a new [product]... I will call next week to see if I can stop by your office, even if you're only available for ten minutes."

## 17. Worth Swiping

- Collapsing self-introduction and the specific ask into the first two sentences for maximum brevity and scannability.
- Pairing a quantified benefit (a specific percentage) with an explicitly minimized time-cost ask in the same breath.
- Having the seller commit to doing the legwork of follow-up ("I will call next week") rather than putting the burden of response on the prospect.
- Offering open-ended, low-pressure assistance as a secondary soft touchpoint before any sale is attempted.

## 18. Context-Dependent Elements

- The "new Vice President of Sales" framing is a specific credibility device tied to a role change and would not transfer to a rep without a comparable claim to authority or novelty.
