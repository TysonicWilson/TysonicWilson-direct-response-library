## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens by voicing the reader's own likely negative reaction to the product's core premise ("my first reaction was, 'Who wants to?'") before revealing that the actual promise differs from that initial, dismissive assumption.
**Likely Reason:** Naming and validating a reader's probable skepticism first builds rapport and makes the subsequent reveal feel like a genuine discovery rather than a forced sales claim, since the writer appears to have shared and then overcome the same doubt.
**General Rule:** When a product's surface-level promise is likely to trigger an immediate, specific objection or negative association, consider opening by voicing that exact objection in the writer's own voice before resolving it, rather than asserting the benefit directly and risking the reader silently supplying the objection unaddressed.
**Use When:** The target objection is common, specific, and genuinely resolvable by a distinction the product legitimately offers.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The objection voiced is a strawman not genuinely representative of the reader's actual concern, which can read as manipulative rather than authentic.
**Example From This Promotion:** "I myself am middle aged. I really enjoy life. But when I heard that Dr. Hrachovec could help me live past 100, my first reaction was, 'Who wants to?'"

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter repeatedly cites specific page numbers throughout its body copy ("On page 129," "pages 141-142," "pages 149-156," "pages 51-52") rather than only summarizing the book's content in general terms.
**Likely Reason:** Specific, consistent page citations imply a genuinely organized, substantive, navigable text, increasing perceived credibility and value even though the reader cannot verify the actual page content before purchase.
**General Rule:** When selling an information product, citing specific internal locations (page numbers, chapter numbers, section titles) for each claim can make the product's value feel more concrete and substantiated than general content summaries alone.
**Use When:** The underlying product is genuinely structured and the citations are accurate, so the reader's eventual experience matches the implied organization.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The product's actual content is not well-organized or citation-worthy, in which case the device could create a mismatch between promise and delivery upon purchase.
