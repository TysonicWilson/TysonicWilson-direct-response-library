## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens with a complete, followable recipe the reader can physically make, before any mention of the magazine, product, or offer.
**Likely Reason:** Having the reader actually perform a small, pleasurable action tied to the product's core benefit creates direct sensory and emotional buy-in before any persuasive claim is made, making the subsequent pitch feel like an extension of an experience already begun rather than a cold sales approach.
**General Rule:** When a product's value is fundamentally experiential (taste, lifestyle, sensation), consider opening with a small, complete, participatory demonstration of that experience rather than a description or claim about it.
**Use When:** The product's benefit can be genuinely and briefly demonstrated in a way the reader can act on immediately with common materials.

**Likely Reason:** Short, direct questions invite active reader self-assessment ("do I enjoy this?") before each content burst, keeping a long, dense proof section scannable and personally engaging rather than a single undifferentiated block of claims.
**General Rule:** When presenting a long list of varied proof points or content categories, consider breaking them into short question-based subheads that invite the reader to self-select relevance before each section.
**Use When:** The underlying content genuinely spans multiple distinct categories worth separating for scannability.
