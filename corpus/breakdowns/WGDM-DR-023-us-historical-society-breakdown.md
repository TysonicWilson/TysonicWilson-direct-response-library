## 8. Mechanism Analysis

The stated "how it works" is a claimed *medium translation*: a photograph → an existing sculpture → now, for the first time, a stained glass art plate, executed by a named artist (Jack Woodson) and produced by a named craftsmen's guild via a specific, described process (15 separate colors applied to cathedral glass rounds, then fired so the colors "become fused forever with the glass"). The mechanism's persuasive job is twofold: (1) establish that stained glass is the *logical* medium for this specific image (via the "Link with Immortality" durability argument and the sculpture-to-glass lineage), and (2) establish that *this specific production* is executed with expert, almost artisanal rigor (named artist, named guild, described firing process) rather than being a mass-produced commercial item — directly supporting the letter's core objection-handling task (Section 11).

## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle 1

**Observation:** Before making a proclamation-style claim about a solemn or emotionally loaded historical subject, the letter opens with a dedication addressed to the people most likely to feel proprietary or protective of that subject (veterans, their families, patriots).
**Likely Reason:** A dedication signals reverence and shared values before the sales argument begins, defusing the specific objection that the offer is commercially exploiting something sacred.
**General Rule:** When a promotion commercializes emotionally sensitive or culturally sacred subject matter, address the most likely objection (exploitation, disrespect) explicitly and structurally — before the sales claim, not after it.
**Use When:** The product is built around historical, religious, military, tragedy-adjacent, or otherwise emotionally weighted source material.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The subject matter carries no such sensitivity — an unnecessary dedication device would read as manufactured solemnity and slow down a lead that could otherwise move directly into benefit or offer.
**Example From This Promotion:** "To anyone who fought in the Great War... To anyone whose loved one was there... To anyone who has pride in America... We dedicate this extraordinary work of art," preceding the "Dear Member" salutation.

### Principle 2

**Observation:** The letter argues not just that the *image* is significant, but that the specific *medium* (stained glass) is the historically and artistically "logical" home for that image, using an independent durability claim about the medium itself (600-year-old stained glass) before returning to the product.
**Likely Reason:** Grounding product legitimacy in an argument about the medium's own track record, separate from the specific item being sold, makes the product's claimed permanence feel inherited rather than asserted.
**General Rule:** When introducing an unfamiliar production medium or format for a familiar subject, establish credibility for the *medium itself* as a discrete argument, before asking the reader to accept the specific execution.
**Use When:** A promotion is translating a known subject into an unconventional or newly-applied production format (a photograph into stained glass, a story into a different collectible form).
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The medium is already the reader's default expectation for the product category — the argument would be redundant and slow the letter down.
**Example From This Promotion:** "Stained Glass... the Link with Immortality" — "Some of the classic stained glass masterpieces created 600 years ago are as radiant and brilliant as they were the day the medieval stained glass artisans lovingly completed them."
