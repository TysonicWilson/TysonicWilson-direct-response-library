## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter opens with, and structures its entire lead around, a long, specifically-titled itemized list (33 articles) rather than a general description of content quality.
**Likely Reason:** A specific, scannable list lets the reader personally verify relevance to their own life across a wide range of items, functioning as self-generated, individually credible proof rather than an asserted claim the reader must simply trust.
**General Rule:** When a product's value comes from breadth and variety (a content library, a feature set, a product catalog), consider leading with a long, specific, itemized list the prospect can personally scan for relevance, rather than summarizing that breadth in general terms.
**Use When:** The underlying inventory of specific items is genuinely large and varied enough to make the list itself feel substantial and credible, and enough items are likely to be personally relevant to a broad readership.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The underlying content/feature set is actually narrow or repetitive, in which case a long list would expose thinness rather than demonstrate breadth.
**Example From This Promotion:** The itemized 33-article list, spanning tax law, home buying, liability insurance, home accidents, and more, presented before any price or offer is mentioned.

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter anchors its discounted price against two distinct reference prices (single-copy-equivalent cost and regular subscription rate) and states the exact dollar savings against each, twice, at different points in the letter.
**Likely Reason:** Providing two different, concrete reference points (rather than one) and repeating the exact savings figures reinforces the value claim through redundancy and gives price-sensitive readers multiple angles from which the discount appears substantial.
**General Rule:** When anchoring a discounted price, consider providing more than one distinct, calculable reference price (e.g., retail-equivalent and regular list price) and stating the exact savings against each, repeating the figures near the close.
**Use When:** Both reference prices are genuine, verifiable, and meaningfully higher than the offered price.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The reference prices are inflated, rarely actually paid by real customers, or only marginally higher than the offer price, which would undermine rather than reinforce the value claim.
**Example From This Promotion:** "You save $5.43 from the single-copy cost, and $2.86 from the regular subscription value."
