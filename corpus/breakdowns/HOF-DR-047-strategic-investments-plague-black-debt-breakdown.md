## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle: Using the Opposition's Own Data as a Second, Reinforcing Proof Point

**Observation:** Rather than relying solely on the newsletter's own debt projections, the piece deliberately includes the Clinton administration's own official budget-plan debt projection as a middle proof point, positioned between historical actual data and the newsletter's own more alarming forecast.
**Likely Reason:** When an argument might be dismissed as one-sided or partisan, showing that even the "other side's" own data supports the same directional conclusion makes the claim significantly harder to dismiss as biased fear-mongering, because the reader cannot attribute the alarming trend to the newsletter's own agenda alone.
**General Rule:** When making a controversial or high-stakes forecasting claim, actively seek out and include a credible, independently-sourced or opposition-sourced data point that supports the same directional conclusion as the promotion's own claim — this reinforces credibility precisely because it cannot be dismissed as self-serving.
**Use When:** A genuine, independently-verifiable data point exists that supports the promotion's thesis and can be attributed to a source the skeptical reader would not suspect of bias in the promotion's favor.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** No such genuinely independent supporting data exists — fabricating or misattributing a data source to simulate this effect would be both a credibility risk and, in a regulated financial-promotion context, a significant compliance risk.
**Example From This Promotion:** The sequential use of the Clinton administration's own budget projection chart between the historical debt chart and the newsletter's own more severe projection.
