## 16. Reusable Principles

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter explicitly connects the referral ask to a specific, personal benefit the reader already receives (lower prices resulting from the company's no-advertising business model), rather than framing the ask as purely helping the company.
**Likely Reason:** Reframing a favor request as mutually beneficial, rather than one-sided, increases the reader's motivation to comply by giving them a self-interested reason (maintaining low prices) in addition to any goodwill-based motivation.
**General Rule:** When asking an existing customer for a favor (referrals, reviews, etc.), explicitly connect the ask to a specific, tangible benefit the customer already receives as a result of the underlying business practice, rather than framing the request as purely benefiting the company.
**Use When:** A genuine, honest causal connection exists between the requested favor and a benefit the customer actually receives (as with a real no-advertising, referral-dependent pricing structure).
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** No genuine causal connection exists between the favor and a customer benefit, in which case the reframing would be misleading rather than honestly persuasive.
**Example From This Promotion:** "As you know, we don't spend money on advertising. Instead, we depend almost entirely on our satisfied clients and customers referring their friends to us. This keeps our marketing costs to a minimum. This is one way we keep our prices low."

### Principle

**Observation:** The letter offers a specific, tangible, unconditional reward (event tickets) for completing the ask, explicitly not contingent on the referral actually converting into a sale.
**Likely Reason:** Making the reward unconditional removes the customer's risk of doing the favor "for nothing" if their friends don't ultimately buy, increasing willingness to participate compared to a reward structure that depends on downstream conversion.
**General Rule:** When requesting a favor with an uncertain, delayed, or hard-to-verify outcome (like whether a referral converts), offer an unconditional reward tied to completing the immediate requested action itself, not to the eventual outcome.
**Use When:** The immediate requested action (e.g., completing a form) is itself the genuinely valuable outcome the company needs, regardless of downstream conversion.
**Do Not Blindly Use When:** The company genuinely needs to incentivize conversion outcomes rather than the referral action itself, in which case an unconditional reward could attract low-quality, unmotivated referrals.
